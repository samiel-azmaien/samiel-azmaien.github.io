CEO / AGENT IDENTITY

Totem

Website ↗

A network where AI agents from different platforms, users, and organizations can communicate and work together.

My contribution

I lead the product and customer discovery. I help companies choose which workflows we will initially support and define success criteria of the test.

The hard part

As agents operate in different companies, each request involves a different set of permissions. We tie the agent’s proof to the company receiving the request and to the particular request itself. In case the permission runs out of time or is revoked, the next check must deny access even if an earlier request succeeded.

What I learned

We start building our network by helping two companies conduct one useful interaction. The early discussions made it easier for me to focus on this: find the recipient of the request, give an example based on his real job and decide which requests must succeed and which must fail.

Current progress

I was selected for Entrepreneurs First's Selection Day. We have paid pilots running and are talking to a16z, Founders Inc. Blueprint, Alliance (ALL19) and Drive Capital.

02

RESEARCH / GPU MEMORY SYSTEMS

GPU–HBM Predictive Caching

Repository ↗ Paper ↗ ISEF ↗

A predictive memory-controller concept that learns memory-block transitions with a Markov chain, validates them with a neural network, and uses quantization and pruning to keep the predictor lightweight.

My contribution

I co-developed the original ISEF research project, designed the predictive-caching methodology and experiments, and later turned the work into a sole-authored journal article. I am also a co-inventor on the granted patent covering the GPU project.

The hard part

A predictor can erase its own benefit if inference and memory overhead are too high. I had to treat model compression, prediction quality, and transfer latency as one systems problem rather than separate metrics.

What I learned

Systems research is only persuasive when the benchmark, simulator assumptions, and available artifacts are stated plainly. The public repository therefore distinguishes the publication record from a complete reproduction package.

Result / traction

The paper reports an 85% L1 cache hit rate, 40% lower memory overhead, and 25% faster data transfer. It was published in IJSCE in January 2026, grew from a Regeneron ISEF project, and resulted in a granted patent.

  • Markov chains
  • Neural networks
  • Quantization
  • Pruning
  • GPU architecture
03

PUBLISHED RESEARCH / RESPONSIBLE ML

FND MRI Classification

Repository ↗ Manuscript ↗

A research prototype testing whether tabular MRI-derived quality metrics could help distinguish neurological-disorder and Functional Neurological Disorder cases.

My contribution

I prepared and standardized the tabular feature pipeline, trained logistic-regression and two-hidden-layer dense-network baselines, generated classification metrics and plots, and documented the work in the accompanying manuscript.

The hard part

The public sample is extremely small and does not reproduce the larger experiment described in the paper. The key challenge became separating an exploratory research result from a claim that could be interpreted as clinical validation.

What I learned

Medical ML needs patient-independent splits, provenance, calibrated baselines, confidence intervals, and error analysis before accuracy is meaningful. A limitation stated clearly is more useful than a headline metric without context.

Result / status

The manuscript and code are public, but the project is labeled research-only—not a medical device or diagnostic tool. The repository documents which results are and are not reproducible from the released sample.

04

DATA SCIENCE / GEOSPATIAL ANALYSIS

Swing-State Probabilities

Repository ↗

An exploratory analysis of 2020–2024 election results that maps transparent, precinct-level swing scores across Gwinnett County.

My contribution

I built the Jupyter workflow for data preparation, regression experiments, sigmoid-based scoring, and raster/geospatial visualization, then recorded the process in a 34-page development logbook.

The hard part

Election tables, precinct geography, and Colab-hosted files did not share a clean schema or reproducible path structure. I also had to avoid presenting a heuristic score as a calibrated forecast.

What I learned

A polished map can hide weak methodology. Reproducible inputs, independent labels, train/test design, and plain-language interpretation matter as much as the model.

Result / status

The notebooks and full logbook are public. The repository explicitly labels the output as exploratory and lists the data, environment, and validation work required for a reproducible release.

  • pandas
  • GeoPandas
  • Rasterio
  • TensorFlow
  • Jupyter
05

CONCLUDED / HACKLANTA 2026

Andromeda

Demo ↗ Archive ↗

A four-person Hacklanta prototype exploring how prediction-market data could become clearer research signals, paper-trading workflows, and strategy tests.

My contribution

As one member of the four-person team, I helped shape and ship the browser prototype that combined market views, probability-driven analysis, paper trading, and strategy exploration. I now maintain the public project record.

The hard part

Prediction markets change quickly, so the interface had to make uncertain signals understandable without implying certainty or live financial advice. Team ownership also meant respecting the boundary around the private source repository.

What I learned

Fast team projects need explicit ownership, provenance, and lifecycle labels. A good archive can preserve the work without misrepresenting who owns the implementation or whether a product is still active.

Result / status

The public frontend remains available as a portfolio artifact. Andromeda is concluded; backend-dependent actions may be unavailable, and the archive intentionally contains no private team source.

  • Prediction markets
  • Paper trading
  • Team prototype
  • Product UX
